# Preview Pal — 3-minute demo script

10 slides, 9 of them narrated. Read at a normal pace and this lands at about **2 minutes 55 seconds**.
Total narration: ~390 words. The same text is in each slide's speaker notes in `PreviewPal.pptx`.

Two slides have a dashed **SCREEN RECORDING** box: slide 6 and slide 7. Everything else is voice only.

---

## 1 · Title — 0:00–0:10

> This is Preview Pal. No surprises — your child's day, pictured before it happens. It is for autistic children, and the families who prepare them.

---

## 2 · A dentist's waiting room — 0:10–0:35

*Slow down here. This is the part the judges will remember.*

> Maya is seven. She is autistic. This morning she is at the dentist, and nobody told her about the light above the chair, or the sound the water makes, or that the person in the mask would count her teeth out loud.
>
> Fifteen minutes later the appointment is abandoned. Her mother rebooks for a month away.
>
> It was not the dentist that did that. It was not knowing.

---

## 3 · This is not a rare problem — 0:35–1:00

> And this is not rare. In the United States, one in thirty-one eight-year-olds is autistic. Worldwide, about sixty-two million people.
>
> Three in four of these children have documented sensory differences — so the lights, the noise and the surprise land much harder. And when a visit goes badly, the care often does not happen at all: fifteen percent have an unmet dental need.

---

## 4 · Families already have the answer — 1:00–1:20

> Families already know the fix. It is called a social story — a short, plain account of what will happen, read to the child beforehand. The research says it works.
>
> But parents hand-make them, one page at a time, with stock clip-art. A cartoon dentist is not their dentist. So most children never get one.

---

## 5 · Three things a parent does — 1:20–1:35

> So we built Preview Pal. A parent writes what will happen, in their own words. We turn it into a short film — with their child's face, their clothes, their comfort toy, and a photograph of the actual room they are about to walk into.

---

## 6 · Making a story — 1:35–2:05  🎬 **RECORDING**

**Record (~30 s):** signing in → opening the child → writing a story → the status moving through *writing → drawing → moving pictures* → the finished video playing.

> Here it is. She writes the steps. Gemini directs each picture under locked rules — this face, this outfit, this room. Veo turns every step into a moving clip, and it is rendered into one video with the narration.
>
> She never sees a model, a prompt, or a pipeline.

---

## 7 · Checked, then approved — 2:05–2:25  🎬 **RECORDING**

**Record (~20 s):** the story page with a flagged picture → pressing approve → the child's calm player running.

> Before any of it reaches Maya, a second model checks every frame against those locks and against the family's must-not-show list — no needles, no drills — and re-shoots what fails.
>
> Then an adult approves it. Only then does the child watch.

---

## 8 · The tenth story takes a sentence — 2:25–2:45

> Everything is remembered in ClickHouse — her look, her places, her sensory profile, and how each real visit actually went. So the first story takes a few minutes, and the tenth takes a sentence.
>
> And when a parent says the waiting room was the hard part, the next story spends more time in the waiting room.

---

## 9 · It is live — 2:45–2:55

> It is live at previewpal dot duckdns dot org. Preview Pal. No surprises.

---

## 10 · Sources — not narrated

Leave it on screen for two seconds, or skip it. It is there so judges can check the figures.

---

# Putting the recordings into the slides

Slides 6 and 7 each have a dashed placeholder box. For each one:

1. Click the dashed box and press **Delete**.
2. **Insert → Video → Video from File** and choose your clip.
3. With the video selected: **Format → Size & Position**, and set
   - Height **4.28"**, Width **7.60"**
   - Position from top-left corner: horizontal **0.75"**, vertical **2.20"**
4. **Playback** tab → Start: **Automatically**.

# Recording the final video

1. **Slide Show → Record Slideshow**, narrate from slide 1 to slide 10. The embedded clips play while you talk.
2. **File → Export → Create a Video** → 1080p → save as MP4.

If you overrun three minutes, cut slide 4 down to its first sentence — it is the most compressible.
